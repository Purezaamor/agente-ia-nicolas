Polymorphism. A restriction fragment length polymor-
phism was identified within PCR amplification products of
the bovine leptin ( lep; formerly known as obese [ob]) gene
using the restriction enzyme Sau3AI.
Primer Description and Sequences. 5′ primer: 5′-GTCAC-
CAGGATCAATGACAT-3′;3′primer: 5′-AGCCCAGGAAT-
GAAGTCCAA-3′.
Method of Detection. The PCR was performed in
10-mL reactions with 10 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.3), 1.95 mM
MgCl2,50mMKCl, 200 mM each dNTP, .165 mM each
primer, .65 units Taq DNA polymerase, and 50 ng genomic
DNA. Thermal cycling conditions included an initial denatu-
ration at 94°C for 2 min, followed by 35 cycles of 94, 58, and
72°C (30 s each), and ending with a final extension for 10
min at 72°C. Digestion of the PCR product (1,820 bp) with
the restriction enzyme Sau3AI revealed a polymorphism
with two alleles (Figure 1).
Inheritance Pattern. Mendelian inheritance of this leptin
PCR-RFLP was confirmed in eight full-sib families (115
offspring) of the International Bovine Reference Panel
(IBRP; Barendse et al., 1994).
Allele Frequencies. Frequencies of the A allele (Figure 1)
in unrelated cattle representing a variety of breeds were .7
in Limousin (n = 5), .79 in Simmental (n = 9), .82 in
Gelbvieh (n = 17), .71 in Holstein (n = 14), .5 in Hereford
(n = 16), .73 in Angus (n = 15), 0 in Brahman (n = 4), .6 in
Brangus (n = 4), and .82 in the parents of the IBRP (n =
30). A rare polymorphism at an additional Sau3AI restric-
tion site (the 690-bp fragment [see Figure 1] was digested
into two fragments of approximately 470 and 220 bp) was
observed in cattle from Simmental, Gelbvieh, and Angus
breeds. This polymorphism was not found in the IBRP and
was not part of the linkage analysis.
Chromosome Location. Linkage analysis (CRI-MAP v2.4
SunOS) using the IBRP and Cattle Genotypic Database
(Barendse et al., 1994) determined that leptin maps to
bovine Chromosome (BTA) 4, linked to markers IDVGA51
and DIK26 (Barendse et al., 1994) with sex-averaged
recombination frequencies of .12 (LOD = 5.94) and .13
(LOD = 3.25), respectively.
Comments. Leptin has been shown to have dramatic
effects on obesity in mice, presumably through changes in
feed intake (Halaas et al., 1995). Assignment of lep to BTA
4 confirms a previous report using linkage analysis of a
Southern blot-RFLP marker (Stone et al., 1996).
