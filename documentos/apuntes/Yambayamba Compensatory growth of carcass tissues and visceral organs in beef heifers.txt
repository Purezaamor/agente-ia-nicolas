Abstract 
Twenty-eight beef heifers (222 + 22 kg SD; 226 rfI 10 d SD on day 0) from a composite breed were used to investigate 
weight recovery of muscle, bone, fat, and visceral organs, and the contribution of gut fill to compensatory growth. Two 
heifers were slaughtered on day 1. Of the remaining 26 heifers, 12 were fed ad libitum (ADLIB) and 14 were given a 
maintenance ration for 92 d, followed by realimentation (REST). They were randomly assigned in pairs to slaughter dates 
and slaughtered over a wide range of live weights (200 to 460 kg). The allometric equation, Y = uXb, was used to study the 
recovery patterns of carcass tissues and visceral organs. Average daily gains were higher (P = 0.001) in REST than in 
ADLIB heifers during realimentation. Feed restriction affected some carcass tissues and visceral organs, but realimentation 
reversed this, and there were no differences in body composition between treatments by about 460 kg live weight. Treatment 
did not affect the allometric growth coefficients (b values) for muscle and bone relative to side weight, but the b value for 
total fat relative to side weight was greater (P = 0.03) in REST than in ADLIB heifers. Relative to total fat, the b values for 
subcutaneous and intermuscular fat were greater (P = 0.01) and smaller (P = 0.03), respectively, in REST than in ADLIB 
heifers. The liver, empty stomach, spleen, and pancreas showed rapid recovery during realimentation of REST heifers. Gut 
till did not contribute to compensatory weight gain. 
Keywords: Beef heifers: Compensatory growth; Carcass tissues; Visceral organs 
1. Introduction 
Realimentation following undernutrition in rumi- 
nants is characterized by very high growth rates 
(Carstens et al., 1989, 1991; Hayden et al., 1993). 
Factors associated with this compensatory growth 
include greater feed intake, feed efficiency, and gut 
fill (Carstens et al., 1989). The major aspect of 
investigation of compensatory growth has been its

2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Animals and experimental design 
Twenty-eight beef heifers from a composite breed 
consisting of approximately 60% Hereford, 12% An- 
gus, 8% Charolais, 5% Galloway, and 15% other 
breeds (Berg et al., 1990) weighing 222 kg (22 kg 
SD) and aged 226 d (10 d SD> on day 0 were used in 
the present study. The heifers were born in April and 
May of 1992 at the University of Alberta Ranch, 
Kinsella. They were weaned in October and trucked 
150 km to Ellerslie Research Station near Edmonton 
35 d prior to the beginning of the experiment. During 
the period of adjustment to the experimental diet 
(Table l), the heifers were fed to appetite and fresh 
water was always available. They were penned in 
groups of seven in open-front shelters with concrete 
floors, and wood shavings were used as bedding 
material. All heifers were weighed on day 0, and 
from them two heifers were randomly selected to be 
slaughtered on day 1 (INIT). Of the remaining 26 
heifers, 12 were allotted to ad libitum feeding (AD-

2.3. Statistical analysis 
3. Results 
Because of the nature of the experiment (serial 
slaughter), the heifers in each treatment were as- 
sumed to be nested within period in order to mean- 
ingfully compute the average daily gains (ADG). 
Dummy variables were assigned to each animal 
(Overall and Klett, 1972) and regression analysis of 
live weights on time was done to compute the ADG. 
Paired t-test using the GLM procedure @AS, 1990) 
was done to assess the difference between the regres- 
sions during the restriction and realimentation peri- 
ods. 
3.1. Growth pe$ormance 
The weight of the carcass side was taken as the 
sum of the weights of its separated components and, 
similarly, total carcass fat weight was the sum of 
individual fat depot weights. Abdominal fat weight 
was the sum of kidney fat, channel fat, and omental 
fat weights. The weights of individual carcass tissues 
and fat depots were expressed as proportions of side 
weights and total fat weight, respectively. The vis- 
ceral organ and abdominal fat weights were ex- 
pressed as proportions of EBW while gut fill was 
expressed as a proportion of FBW. These data were 
subjected to a two-way analysis of variance @AS, 
1990) to compare the treatment effects within period 
and treatment X time interactions. Comparison of 
body composition between INIT heifers and the other 
groups was done only during the restriction period. 
By design, the body weights of REST heifers 
were maintained relatively constant during the feed 
restriction period (Fig. 2); during realimentation, their 
ADG was higher (P = 0.001) than that of ADLIB 
heifers. By day 134 of realimentation, the REST 
heifers had caught up to the ADLIB heifers in live 
weight. The average feed consumption by the REST 
group was also higher than that of the ADLIB group 
during this period, but overall they consumed less 
feed (P = 0.001) than the ADLIB heifers (5436 vs. 
6780 kg).

3.2. Body composition 
3.2. I. Carcass tissues 
The carcass data are presented in Table 2. There 
were treatment (P < 0.001) and time (P < 0.01) ef- 
fects on the proportions of the carcass tissues. There 
was, however, no treatment X time interaction effect 
on any of the tissues. During feed restriction, there 
was no treatment effect (P > 0.05) on the proportion 
of side muscle but the proportion of side bone was 
higher (P < 0.05) in the REST than in the INIT 
group, which in turn was higher than in the ADLIB 
group. Conversely, the proportion of total side fat 
was lower (P < 0.05) in the REST than in the INIT 
group, which was in turn lower than in the ADLIB 
group on both slaughter days. The SCF proportion 
tended to be lower (P < 0.10) in REST than in INIT 
and ADLIB heifers on both days; the IMF proportion 
tended to be higher (P = 0.08) in REST than in INIT 
and ADLIB heifers on day 50, and it was significant 
(P = 0.02) on day 92. 
During realimentation, side muscle proportion did 
not differ between REST and ADLIB heifers, al- 
though there was a tendency (P < 0.10) towards a 
higher proportion in the former than in the latter on 
days 50 and 78. The effect of feed restriction on side 
bone proportion was still evident 29 d into realimen- 
tation. The difference, however, slowly disappeared 
through the course of realimentation and by day 134 
(when the two groups had reached equal live weight), 
no difference between treatments was apparent. Con- 
versely, side fat proportion was lower (P = 0.05) in 
REST than in ADLIB heifers on days 29, 50, and 78 
of realimentation. The proportion of side fat slowly 
increased in both treatments, and at equal live weight, 
there was no difference between treatments. The 
proportion of side SCF was lower (P < 0.05) in 
REST than in ADLIB heifers on days 29 and 50, and 
to a smaller degree (P = 0.09) on day 78 of reali- 
mentation; complete recovery had taken place by day 
134. The side IMF proportion in REST heifers de- 
creased gradually and no significant differences were 
detected between treatments at any time during reali- 
mentation. The side CCF proportion was not affected 
by treatment to any significant degree. 
3.2.2. Visceral organs 
There were significant treatment and time effects 
(P < 0.01) on the proportions of liver, kidneys, 
spleen, and pancreas (Table 3). The proportion of 
liver was lower (P = 0.05) in REST than in ADLIB 
heifers on day 50 of feed restriction, while INIT 
heifers were intermediate. The proportions of heart, 
kidneys, lungs + trachea, spleen, pancreas, and 
adrenal glands were not significantly affected by 
feed restriction. On day 92, the proportions of liver 
(P = 0.04) and spleen (P = 0.01) were lower in 
REST than in INIT and ADLIB heifers. No treat- 
ment differences were observed in the proportions of 
the other organs during this period. 
During realimentation, the proportions of liver 
and spleen in REST heifers recovered rapidly and no 
differences between these heifers and ADLIB heifers 
existed by day 29. On days 50 and 78, the proportion 
of liver in REST heifers was higher (P = 0.01) than 
that of ADLIB heifers. By day 134, however, the 
proportions were similar. This pattern of recovery 
was also evident in the spleen. The absolute weights 
of the livers and spleens (data not shown) were also 
larger (P < 0.051 in REST than in ADLIB heifers 
during the mid-part of the realimentation period. No 
significant effect was observed in the other organs 
except for the proportion of the pancreas which was 
higher (P = 0.05) in REST than in ADLIB heifers 
on day 78. Interactions between nutritional treatment 
and time were significant for the proportions of liver 
(P = 0.02) and spleen (P = 0.011, and these are 
presented in Figs. 3 and 4, respectively.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Growth rates 
During realimentation, the ADG of REST heifers 
was higher than that of ADLIB heifers, particularly 
during the first 6 to 8 weeks, with some individual 
heifers gaining up to 2.8 kg/d. Compensation in live 
weight was complete by day 134 of realimentation. 
Similar results have been reported by Yambayamba 
and Price (1991) and Carstens et al. (1989). Some 
reports have suggested that gut fill contributes to 
apparent compensatory growth in cattle (Baker et al., 
1985; Carstens et al., 1989). Others have attributed it 
to increased efficiency of energy and protein utiliza- 
tion (Fox et al., 19721, increased feed intake, de- 
creased maintenance requirement, and changes in the 
composition of gain (Carstens et al., 1989). The 
present data do not suggest any significant contribu- 
tion of gut fill to compensatory growth. Thus, the 
observed high growth rates could be attributed to the 
actual increased efficiency of nutrient utilization. 
4.2. Carcass composition 
Fat, being the most variable tissue in the carcass, 
has a large influence on the proportions of bone and 
muscle (Berg and Butterfield, 1976). In the present 
study, the lower proportion of total side fat during 
feed restriction was associated with a higher propor- 
tion of side bone but similar proportion of side 
muscle in REST heifers compared to ADLIB and 
INIT heifers. Price (1977) observed the same trend 
in restricted steers. Within the fat tissue, fat depots 
were affected differentially by feed restriction. Berg 
and Butterfield (1976) observed that fat depots fol- 
low an order of depletion during feed restriction, 
opposite to the direction of their development. In this 
situation, the undernourished animal would first uti- 
lize the SCF depot, which was the tendency in the 
present study. The higher proportion of side IMF 
observed in REST heifers compared to ADLIB 
heifers and the non-significance between treatments 
in terms of side CCF proportion were a reflection of 
the greater mobilization of the SCF depot in the 
restricted animals. 
During realimentation of the REST heifers, there 
was an increase in the proportion of total side fat 
over time, causing side muscle and bone proportions 
to decrease as full feeding progressed. By day 29 of 
realimentation, the proportion of total side fat in 
REST heifers had increased by 22% from that in 
heifers slaughtered at the end of the restriction pe- 
riod (day 92). By day 134, there were no differences 
in the side carcass composition between treatments. 
These results agree with a preliminary study 
(Yambayamba and Price, 19911, in which carcass 
composition was estimated from 3-rib joint dissec- 
tions. The effect of realimentation on fat depots was 
an increase in the proportion of SCF and a decrease 
in the proportion of IMF in REST heifers, opposite 
to the trends observed during feed restriction. Reali- 
mentation, however, did not significantly affect CCF 
proportion. These results are consistent with those 
reported in steers (Tatum et al., 1986): with increas- 
ing total fat in the side, the proportion of IMF 
declined slightly while the proportion of SCF in- 
creased and that of internal fat remained relatively 
constant. 
4.3. Visceral organs 
Among the visceral organs studied, only the liver 
and the spleen were significantly affected by feed 
restriction. Since the liver is the centre for intermedi- 
ary metabolism (Stangassinger and Giesecke, 19861, 
feed restriction would be expected to decrease its 
metabolic activity and size (But-tin et al., 1990). 
Several studies in ruminants (Reid et al., 1980; Fer- 
rell et al., 1986; Aziz et al., 1993) have reported 
decreases in the absolute and relative size of the 
liver. The lower proportion of spleen in REST heifers 
on day 92 of feed restriction was also expected. 
Seebeck (1967) found similar results in steers, and 
since one of the functions of the spleen is to store 
blood for release under stressful conditions, he sug- 
gested that the lower spleen weight may have been 
related to a concomitant reduction in blood weight. 
It was not clear why feed restriction did not have 
a significant effect on the proportion of kidneys. 
These results do not agree with some reported for 
lambs (Ferrell et al., 1986; Aziz et al., 1993) but do 
agree with those observed in beef heifers 
(Yambayamba and Price, 1992) subjected to 2 and 4 
months of feed restriction. It may be speculated that 
feed restriction in the present study was not severe 
E.S. Yambayamba et al. /Livestock Production Science 46 (1996) 19-32 29 
enough to cause any significant effect on these or- 
gans. The proportions of heart and lungs + trachea 
were not altered by feed restriction. This was not 
surprising, since the work performed by these organs 
is proportional to the size of the body. The propor- 
tion of pancreas was also unaffected by feed restric- 
tion, contrary to the results observed by Aziz et al. 
(1993) in lambs. Aziz et al. (1993) interpreted their 
finding in terms of the role the pancreas plays in the 
secretion of digestive fluids. They noted that the 
reduction in this organ’s weight during weight loss 
seemed logical since feed intake normally declines 
during weight loss. 
During realimentation, there was a rapid response 
in the proportions of the liver and spleen of REST 
heifers. By day 29 of realimentation, the liver pro- 
portion in REST heifers had already caught up with 
that of ADLIB heifers, and was higher by day 50 
(Fig. 3). The higher liver proportion in REST heifers 
was observed for a greater part of the realimentation 
period. A similar pattern of recovery was observed in 
the proportion of spleen (Fig. 4). Since the spleen 
stores blood, and since there is an increased blood 
flow to the portal drained viscera during feed intake 
(Lapierre et al., 19921, it may be concluded that the 
rapid response of the spleen during realimentation of 
REST heifers was due to increased cardiac output 
and increased capacity to store blood by this organ. 
The other organs were not affected by treatment 
during realimentation, except for the pancreas which 
was at a higher proportion in REST than in ADLIB 
heifers on day 78. 
4.4. Gastrointestinal tract and abdominal fat 
The lag in the response of empty stomach and 
intestines to nutrition was unexpected since the GIT, 
like the liver, normally responds very rapidly to 
nutritional demands (Murray et al., 1977). On the 
other hand, the lack of a significant effect by feed 
restriction on abdominal fat agrees with the concept 
that internal fat is least affected by feed restriction 
(Berg and Butterfield, 1976). 
Realimentation of REST heifers was characterized 
by a rapid weight recovery in both empty stomach 
and empty intestines. The lower proportion (P = 
0.03) of intestines in REST than in ADLIB heifers 
on day 134 is unexplained. Nutritional treatment x 
time interactions were significant for the proportion 
of stomach, suggesting a temporary over compensa- 
tion in this organ (Fig. 5) in the form of an over-use 
hypertrophy. On the other hand, the response of 
abdominal fat to realimentation was slow, with its 
proportion in REST heifers being lower than in 
ADLIB heifers for a greater part of the period. From 
these results, it is concluded that there is a lag in the 
response of internal fat to the nutritional treatment. 
4.5. Gut fZ1 
The larger amount and proportion of gut fill 
observed in REST heifers during the restriction pe- 
riod was an interesting finding. It is commonly 
expected that feed restriction would be associated 
with significantly smaller amounts of gut fill com- 
pared with ad libitum feeding (Baker et al., 1985; 
Carstens et al., 1989). However, Okine and Mathison 
(199 1) and Luginbuhl et al. (1994) found that low 
feed intakes in cattle were associated with reduced 
rate of passage through the GIT, resulting in an 
increase in total gut content as observed in the 
present study. 
The weight of gut contents relative to live weight 
in REST heifers continued to decline during reali- 
mentation, indicating that compensatory growth was 
not due to gut fill. These results do not agree with 
the views of Hogg (1991) who, in his review, con- 
cluded that the rapidly changing gut fill in 
restricted-refed ruminants could make a substantial 
contribution to the apparent increase in live weight 
gain. 
4.6. Allomeq 
4.6.1. Carcass tissues and fat depots 
The present growth coefficients for side muscle 
during realimentation of REST heifers are similar to 
those of other heifers restricted for 2 months fol- 
lowed by realimentation (Yambayamba and Price, 
199 1). Although no differences were found between 
ADLIB and REST for side muscle growth coeffi- 
cients, the b value for the ADLIB heifers was 
significantly smaller than 1.0 while that for the 
REST heifers was not different from 1.0. These 
results parallel the results of Murray et al. (1974) and 
suggest that muscle was probably growing absolutely 
30 ES. Yambayamba et al. / Livestock Production Science 46 (1996) 19-32 
and relatively faster in the restricted-refed heifers 
than in the ADLIB heifers. This scenario would 
confirm the claim that compensatory growth was real 
rather than being attributable to gut fill. In fact, 
others have shown significant increases in muscle 
protein (Hayden et al., 1993), RNA (Howarth and 
Baldwin, 1971), and DNA (Trenkle et al., 1978) 
contents in restricted-refed animals. 
The growth coefficients for side bone relative to 
side weight were similar in both treatments and 
significantly smaller than 1.0. This was expected 
since nutritional restriction has been shown to have 
little effect on bone growth in cattle (Murray et al., 
1974; Price, 1977; Yambayamba and Price, 1991). 
While the growth coefficients for total side fat rela- 
tive to side weight in both treatments were greater 
than 1.0, the b value for the restricted-refed heifers 
was greater than that of the ad libitum fed heifers. 
This was as expected, since fat is the most labile 
carcass tissue. Despite the higher b value for side fat 
in REST heifers during realimentation, the carcass 
composition was similar to ADLIB heifers at about 
460 kg, presumably because the period of fat deposi- 
tion was so shortened that total fat deposition did not 
exceed that in the ADLIB heifers. 
Considering the relative growth of side fat depots, 
the SCF depot was accumulating relatively faster in 
REST heifers than in ADLIB heifers while the other 
two depots were unaffected. The growth coefficients 
found for SCF and IMF in the unrestricted heifers of 
the present study are similar to those reported by 
Tatum et al. (1986). It can be concluded from these 
results that during realimentation, most of the fat 
accumulation in REST heifers was stored in the 
subcutaneous depot. 
4.6.2. Visceral organs and abdominal fat 
The greater growth coefficients for liver, spleen, 
pancreas, and stomach weights in REST than in 
ADLIB heifers during the realimentation period indi- 
cated a great capacity for recovery of these organs. 
These results suggest a higher metabolic activity in 
these organs in the restricted-refed heifers compared 
to the control heifers. The b values for heart, kid- 
neys, and lungs + trachea were similar in both treat- 
ments and were all smaller than 1.0 except for 
lungs + trachea (b not different from 1 .O> in REST 
heifers. These results suggest that these organs do 
not respond to nutritional demands to a significant 
degree as in the case of the liver and GIT. Similar 
results were reported for steers (Seebeck, 1967). 
While the b values for abdominal fat were greater 
than 1.0 in both treatments, its accumulation tended 
to be faster in REST than in ADLIB heifers, eventu- 
ally catching up by day 134. 
5. Conclusions 
Feed restriction followed by realimentation had 
no effect on body composition at equal live weight 
(about 460 kg) of beef heifers in the present study. 
While feed restriction resulted in significant changes 
in the proportions of some carcass tissues and vis- 
ceral organs, realimentation was associated with rapid 
recovery of these tissues and organs. The results 
suggested that the relative growth of muscle was 
higher in the restricted-refed than in the unrestricted 
heifers, presumably because of muscle’s primary 
functional role in relation to live weight, a concept 
that Price (1977) has called ‘homeomyosis’. Al- 
though fat accumulation was faster in the realiment- 
ing than in the unrestricted heifers, the time of 
complete recovery was so shortened that there was 
no significant difference between treatments at the 
end of the study. Regarding the visceral organs, 
compensatory growth has been associated with very 
rapid responses of the liver and the GIT. Although 
these two organs constitute less than 10% of body 
mass (Ferrell, 1988), they account for a major pro- 
portion of total energy expenditure, and their acceler- 
ated growth during compensatory growth is a re- 
sponse to greater feed intake and higher efficiency of 
feed utilization. In fact, Anugwa and Pond (1989) 
stated that an increased rate of growth of these 
metabolically active organ systems, despite the asso- 
ciated obligatory increase in energy expenditure to 
accommodate their increased size, is suggested as a 
possible underlying requisite for compensatory 
growth of the whole body during realimentation 
following feed restriction. 
Finally, although some studies have shown that 
gut fill substantially contributes to compensatory 
growth in ruminants, the present study showed that 
there was no contribution from gut fill. It is con- 
cluded that compensatory growth is a function of

actual tissue growth and has no negative effects on 
body composition. It could be used to advantage in 
ruminant production in saving feed costs.
