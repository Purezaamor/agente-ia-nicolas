Introduction
Meat deposition capacity and growth rate of cattle
and pigs is related to muscle ﬁber numbers (Swatland
and Kiefer, 1974; Hanset et al., 1982; Handel and
Stickland, 1984, 1988). Muscle ﬁber formation takes
place during embryonic development regulated by the
MyoD gene family, which consists of four genes, MyoD1,
myogenin, myf-5, and myf-6 (MRF4) (Olson, 1990;
Montarras et al., 1991; Weintraub et al., 1991; Lyons
and Buckingham, 1992). The myf-5 and MyoD1 genes
are involved in myoblast proliferation. Myogenin ex-
pression is associated with differentiation (i.e., fusion
of mononucleated myoblasts into multinucleated myo-

Postnatal expression of the MyoD1, myf-5, and myo-

after muscle injury (Fuchtbauer and Westphal, 1992;
Grounds et al., 1992). Postnatal MyoD1, myf-5, and

mRNA expression seem to be informative for the num-

1992), which could be related to muscle growth rate.
Therefore, these genes could have a major effect on
muscularity and growth (te Pas and Visscher, 1994).

Materials and Methods
Animals
Animals were treated and slaughtered according to
Dutch governmental regulations dealing with the treat-
ment of livestock.
Pigs were from two experimental Large White selec-
tion lines originating from one commercial population.
Pigs in the fast (F)-line were selected for high growth
rate, and pigs in the lean (L)-line were selected for
leanness (i.e., for reduced backfat thickness). Pigs were
selected on their own performance only. At the time of
slaughter, the lines had been selected for an average
of 2.8 generations. Five boars were randomly taken
from each selection line and slaughtered at the same
average age. The two lines differ considerably in their
tissue deposition characteristics (Sonesson et al., 1998;
see also Table 1). The lifetime daily gain was 8% higher
in the F-line than in the L-line, and the lean tissue
daily growth rate was 6.6% higher in the L-line. The
slaughter weight of the pigs in the F-line was 8.9%
higher than the slaughter weight of the pigs of the L-
line, and the lean mass was 2.8% higher in the L-line.
The animals were characterized for their phenotypic
performance measured by determination of their
weight at 170 d of age (test weight) and backfat thick-
ness adjusted to a common body weight (i.e., test
backfat).
Muscle Tissue Samples
Boars were slaughtered, and muscle biopsies were
taken directly after bleeding, before further processing
of the carcass. Biopsies were taken from longissimus
(LON); gastrocnemius (GAS), caput laterale; soleus
(SOL); supraspinatus (SUP); infraspinatus (INF); sar-
torius (SAR); adductor magnus (ADM); semimembra-
nosus (SEM); trapezius (TRA); psoas major (PSM), and
semitendinosus (white part and red part; SEW and
SER, respectively) muscles. Muscles were sampled at
the surface, except for the semitendinosus red part,
which is more internally localized. Tissue biopsies were
frozen directly in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C
before isolation of RNA.
RNA Isolation
The RNA was isolated from 100 mg of tissue essen-
tially as described by Chomczynski and Sacchi (1987).
The RNA samples were quantiﬁed spectrophotometri-
cally at 260 nm. All RNA isolates had A260/A280 be-
tween 1.8 and 2.0, indicating pure and clean RNA iso-
lates. The RNA integrity was checked by agarose gel
electrophoresis to ensure that the RNA was intact.
Reverse Transcriptase (RT)-PCR-ELISA
The RT-PCR was performed using a two-step RT-
PCR kit from Perkin Elmer (Norwalk, CT). Random
hexamers were used during the cDNA synthesis. The
PCR-ELISA was performed using a kit from Boehringer
Mannheim (Mannheim, Germany). The RT-PCR-
ELISA was performed essentially as described before
(van den Hemel-Grooten et al., 1997). The PCR primers,
reaction conditions, and 5′ biotin-labeled capture
probes are described in Table 2. The PCR and ELISA
reactions were optimized for quantitative reactions.
First, the PCR was performed with 40 cycles to ensure
a clean PCR reaction with one sharp product band only.
Second, the number of PCR cycles was lowered until a
quantitative signal was obtained on agarose gel electro-
phoresis, which was veriﬁed by ELISA (see below). The
RT-PCR-ELISA was optimally quantitative with 24 and

Veriﬁcation of Quantitativeness
of the RT-PCR-ELISA of Each Reaction
Two independent PCR reactions, differing by two cy-
cles, were performed. The PCR cycles were in the quan-
titative range. Aliquots of each PCR product (10 µL)
were used in the ELISA. Furthermore, the mix was
diluted four times. In the quantitative range of the PCR,
a difference of two cycles suggests that the reaction
product of the PCR with the highest cycle number con-
tains four times more reaction product than the PCR
with the lower cycle number. Thus, the ELISA from
the four times diluted high cycle PCR reaction should
be similar to the ELISA of the undiluted low cycle
PCR reaction.
Part of the analyses was done in duplicate. Overall
repeatability of the entire procedure varied between 80
and 98%, depending on the gene under investigation.
Statistical Evaluation
The results are presented as means ± SE. The mRNA
expression levels between lines were statistically evalu-
ated using a t-test approach. To account for the effect
of slaughter age on the traits, expression levels were
preadjusted to a standard age at slaughter within line
using a linear regression (SAS, 1989). To study system-
atic effects of mRNA expression (60 observations per
gene per line, ﬁve animals, and 12 muscles) over mus-
cles, the adjusted expression levels were subsequently
analyzed with SAS (1989) for each selection line sepa-
rately with the model Y = µ + animal + muscle + residual
error. Systematic effects were evaluated with a rank
correlation test using SAS (1989).

Relationship Between Relative Muscle-Speciﬁc
Growth Rate and mRNA Expression
The growth rate of individual muscles is not always
equal to the overall growth rate of the entire animal.
Thus, each individual muscle has a speciﬁc relative
growth rate that is expressed as the b-value of the mus-
cle, the speciﬁc growth rate of the muscle as compared
to the overall growth rate of the animal, which is set
to 1. Thus, a b-value below 1 indicates that the speciﬁc
muscle is growing more slowly than the entire animal,
and a b-value higher than 1 indicates that the muscle
is growing relatively faster than the entire animal (allo-
metric approach, see Huxley, 1932). Because the results
described above suggested that the mRNA expression
levels could be related to selection for high growth (F-
line), we investigated the possible relationship between
the muscle-speciﬁc growth rate or b-values and the
mRNA expression levels in the muscles. The b-values
were taken from Richmond and Berg (1982). The values
are speciﬁc for the Large White breed.
Figure 2 shows the relationship between the b-values
for the muscles and the β-actin normalized mRNA ex-
pression levels of the genes of the MyoD gene family
for muscle biopsies taken from pigs of the F-line (Figure
2A) and from pigs of the L-line (Figure 2B). The muscles
are ordered with increasing b-values along the hori-
zontal axes. The bars representing the RT-PCR-ELISA
results are not parallel with the b-values.
The mRNA expression data were statistically ana-
lyzed for systematic effects among muscles related to
b-values using a rank correlation test (Table 4). A sys-
tematic effect of muscle was found for myf-6 in the
F-line; muscles with higher b-values correlated with
increased myf-6 expression level (rank correlation .38,
P = .067). This indicates that myf-6 expression is depen-
dent on the muscle in which the expression is measured.
Surprisingly, expression levels of all four genes per
muscle seem to be related; most muscles with higher
expression levels than other muscles have higher ex-
pression of all four genes. However, this effect is not
related to the b-value of the individual muscles, as visu-
alized in Figure 2.
Relationship Between Muscle Fiber Type
Composition and mRNA Expression
A relationship between myoﬁber type and MyoD1 and
myogenin mRNA expression levels in adult rat muscle
tissue has been described (Hughes et al., 1993), in which
MyoD1 is expressed mainly in white muscles and myo-
genin in red muscles. Because meat color is an im-
portant meat quality trait that is related to muscle ﬁber
type composition, we investigated the myogenin:MyoD1
mRNA expression ratio in the pig muscles in both selec-
tion lines in relation to muscle color. Muscles biopsies
with a red appearance are (ordered in declining color
intensity) INF, SUP, ADM, TRA, SER, and PSM, and
LON, SEM, and SEW are white-colored muscles. Other
muscles are more intermediately colored.
Table 5 shows the myogenin:MyoD1 ratio for the mus-
cle biopsies taken from pigs of the F-line and of the L-
line. Clearly, the F-line and L-line are very different.
The L-line shows a good correlation between muscle
color and the myogenin:MyoD1 ratio with a higher ratio
for the more red-colored muscles. Such a correlation
was not found for the F-line. Furthermore, Table 4
shows that the myogenin:MyoD1 ratio for each muscle
is higher in muscle biopsies taken from boars of the F-
line than in muscle biopsies taken from boars of the L-
line, except for the longissimus muscle.
Relationships Between Animal Performance
and mRNA Expression
The animals were analyzed for their individual phe-
notypic performance measured as test weight and test
backfat. The within-line association with mRNA ex-
pression levels was investigated. In the F-line myo-
genin level was negatively related to test backfat (esti-
mate =−.177; P = .076). No other signiﬁcant relation-
ships were found (P > .1).
Discussion
The mRNA expression levels of the genes of the MyoD
gene family in pig muscle tissue at slaughter age were
investigated in relation to selection for growth rate and
lean meat deposition. The relation to relative muscle-
speciﬁc growth rate and muscle ﬁber type composition
was also investigated. We used the RT-PCR-ELISA
method to be able to handle large numbers of samples.
This method allowed us to compare the mRNA levels
of samples with each other (Van den Hemel-Grooten et
al., 1997). This method may be vulnerable to contami-
nation of the RNA/cDNA preparations. Therefore, the
use of a constitutively expressed internal control mRNA
is needed. We standardized the mRNA expression of the
genes of the MyoD family against β-actin mRNA level.
Relationship Between Selection for Porcine Growth
Rate and mRNA Expression Level of the Genes
of the MyoD Gene Family: Between-Line Effects
In a number of muscles the myogenin, myf-5, and
MyoD1 mRNA expression levels were higher in muscle
tissue taken from pigs of the F-line, selected for high
growth rate, than in muscle tissue taken from pigs of
the L-line, selected for lean growth (i.e., selected against
backfat thickness). Therefore, these results suggest a
positive relationship between selection for pig growth
rate and myogenin, myf-5, and MyoD1 mRNA expres-
sion at slaughter for a number of muscles.

average contain 32% muscle in the whole body, whereas
L-pigs contain 37% at comparable weight (derived from
data of Sonesson et al., 1998). This suggests that the
expression of the genes of the MyoD gene family is
controlled more by selection for the overall growth rate
of the animals than by selection for muscle deposition
per se. Indeed, the expression of the genes of the MyoD
gene family is regulated by several hormones (Florini
et al., 1991a). In vitro cultures show that myogenin
expression is regulated by IGF-I (Florini et al., 1991b),
and IGF-I expression is regulated by GH. Because GH
is known to inﬂuence animal growth rate (Lund-Larsen
and Bakke, 1975; Spencer, 1985; Etherton, 1988), and
GH level is known to be inﬂuenced by selection for
increased growth rate (Lund-Larsen and Bakke, 1975),
selection for increased growth rate could affect expres-
sion of the genes of the MyoD gene family through the
GH axis. In a preliminary study, Freriksen et al. (1994)
reported that the serum GH level differed between the
selection lines after one generation of selection. Con-
comitantly, IGF-I levels increased, especially in the F-
line (de Greef and te Pas, 1997). Furthermore, pituitary
GH mRNA levels increased signiﬁcantly during three
generations of selection in both lines (de Greef and te
Pas, 1997; te Pas et al., 1998; te Pas et al., unpub-
lished data).
Myogenin, myf-5, and MyoD1 expression levels are
also related to the size of the activated satellite cell
population in mouse muscle tissue (Beilharz et al.,
1992). This suggests that the active satellite cell popula-
tion in muscles of F-line pigs is larger than in muscles

but it ﬁts with the general view that faster growth is

A lower degree of maturity could be associated with a
younger physiological stage of the muscles. This could
be indicative of increased growth potential of the mus-
cles and, thus, of higher possible carcass weights and
delayed maturation of pigs with increased adult weight.
The results also suggest a tendency of myf-6 mRNA
to be expressed more highly in muscle tissue of the
lean selection line, exhibiting a relatively slower growth
pattern. This could be the result of the higher lean mass
of the pigs of the L-line, because thicker muscle ﬁbers
probably express higher levels of myf-6 for main-
tenance.
Within-Line Effects: Relationship Between mRNA
Expression Level and Performance of Individual Pigs
Statistical analyses revealed a negative association
in the F-line myogenin expression level and test backfat
thickness. No such relationship was observed in the L-
line, probably because the range in the traits in the L-
line was much less than in the F-line. Thus, be-
tween-line effects showed myogenin, myf-5, and MyoD1
levels higher in a number of muscles of the faster grow-
ing and heavier F-line pigs, and, within the F-line, the
heavier pigs showed no association with growth rate
and lean mass. Possible explanations could be that the
within-line effect is too small to be signiﬁcant with this
small number of animals. However, even if a correlation
could be shown with larger numbers of pigs, the effect
would be small and probably not relevant for practical
pig breeding.
Relative Muscle-Speciﬁc Growth Rate: Within-
and Between-Line Comparison
The mRNA expression levels of the MyoD genes in
each muscle at slaughter were analyzed for association
with the b-value of the muscle. The b-values are muscle-
speciﬁc growth rate characteristics relative to total
growth rate. Thus, there may be a relationship between
the muscle-speciﬁc expression level of the genes of the
MyoD family and the b-value.
The b-values may vary under several conditions.
First, the b-values may be related to the live weight
range under investigation. However, relative muscle
growth in pigs over the range in live weight is more
monophasic than in cattle and sheep (Richmond and
Berg, 1982). This also suggests that b-values for the
test period and between the test period and slaughter
are similar. Second, the b-values differ between pig
breeds (Davies, 1974; Richmond and Berg, 1982). This
may be related to breed-speciﬁc body conformation.
This may also suggest that b-values may change during
selection. However, considering the Large White-spe-
ciﬁc data, there is much less variation. Data from Davis
(1974) and Richmond and Berg (1982) are fairly compa-
rable, as is also stated by Richmond and Berg (1982).
There may be some variation, but this is mostly related
to the method of dissection of the carcass (i.e., prepara-
tion of individual muscles), and a possible relationship
with carcass muscle percentage was found for only a
few muscles (Richmond and Berg, 1982). However, we
did not use the absolute b-values; we only ranked the
muscles according to their b-values. Comparing the re-
ported experiments for b-values of Large White pigs
(Davies, 1974; Richmond and Berg, 1982; a total of ﬁve
experiments) did not show signiﬁcant differences for
ranking of muscles according to their b-values.
Statistical analyses revealed a positive correlation
between myf-6 mRNA levels and b-values in the F-
line. Thus, faster-growing muscles show higher myf-6
expression. Because myf-6 expression is found only in
mature muscle ﬁbers (Olson, 1990; Montarras et al.,
1991; Weintraub et al., 1991; Lyons and Buckingham,
1992), faster growing muscles may be more mature at
slaughter than slower-growing muscles.
Surprisingly, we did not ﬁnd a relation between b-
values and myogenin, MyoD1, and(or) myf-5 mRNA
levels. This may be another argument for the explana-
tion that hormones related to body growth rate (i.e.,
GH and IGF-I) regulate the expression of the genes of
the MyoD family. Furthermore, this suggests that the
genes of the MyoD family are not the only regulators
of muscle growth rate.
Muscle Fiber Type Composition
and MyoD Gene Expression
Hughes et al. (1993) found that MyoD1 and myogenin
mRNA expression level was related to muscle ﬁber type
in rats. The expression of MyoD1 was speciﬁcally found
in white muscles, and myogenin expression was local-
ized mainly in red muscles. The results described in
this paper support this for muscles from pigs selected
for leanness (L-line), because the color of the muscle
was clearly related to the b-value, but not for muscle
tissue samples taken from pigs selected for growth rate
(F-line). However, Brocks et al. (1997) found that selec-
tion increased the number of Type I (red) ﬁbers and
decreased the number of Type IIB (white) ﬁbers in boars
of the F-line compared to boars of the L-line in a number
of muscles. We found that the myogenin:MyoD1 ratio
was higher in muscle tissue from F-line pigs than in
muscle tissue from L-line pigs, which is in agreement
with Hughes et al. (1993). These results suggest that
selection not only can affect the expression level of the
individual genes in the two selection lines, it can also
differentially affect the level of each individual gene.
Alternatively, selection for growth rate could also differ-
entially affect muscle ﬁber type composition, thereby
inﬂuencing the mRNA levels of the myogenin and

Implications
Increased mRNA expression level of the myogenin,
myf-5, and MyoD1 genes in pigs selected for increased
growth rate may be used as a muscle-speciﬁc indicator
of selection response for increased growth rate. Fur-
thermore, in pigs selected for leanness, the myo-
genin:MyoD1 ratio may be used as an indicator for meat
color/muscle ﬁber composition. However, mRNA ex-
pression levels do not seem to be good indicators of
muscle maturation. Finally, the within-selection-line
association between mRNA expression levels of the
genes of the MyoD gene family and performance traits
is not clear.
