Interaction of GH polymorphism with body weight and
endocrine functions in Japanese black calves
K. Katoh b,∗, S. Kouno a, A. Okazaki b, K. Suzuki b, Y. Obara b
a Hiroshima Prefectural Livestock Technology Research Center, Shobara Hiroshima 727-0023, Japan
b Department of Animal Physiology, Graduate School of Agricultural Science, Tohoku University, Aoba-ku, Sendai 981-8555, Japan
Received 27 June 2006; received in revised form 27 September 2006; accepted 2 October 2006
Abstract
We assessed the interaction of GH gene polymorphisms (AA, AB and BB genotypes) with body weight and measures of endocrine
function in Japanese black calves at 10 months of age. The average body weight for the BB genotype (281 ± 5 kg) was signiﬁcantly
lower (P = 0.0017, ANOVA) than those for the AA (324 ± 9 kg) and AB (317 ± 7 kg) genotypes. Plasma concentrations of insulin
and IGF-I were greater for the AA genotype than for the AB genotype, and AB and BB genotypes, respectively. There were
signiﬁcant differences in the triglyceride and cholesterol concentrations among the GH genotypes. The area under the basal GH
concentration was signiﬁcantly greater (P = 0.0314) for the AA genotype than for the two other genotypes. The incremental area
over the basal GH concentrations in response to intravenous GHRH injection (0.4 /H9262g/kg BW) was signiﬁcantly smaller (P = 0.0005)
for the BB genotype than for the two other genotypes. In addition, linear regression analysis between GH incremental area induced
by GHRH and body weight demonstrated that there was a positive linear correlation (r = 0.6496, P < 0.002) for incremental areas
less than 600 ng min/ml, but a negative correlation (r = 0.6473, P < 0.05) for incremental areas over 600 ng min/ml. These ﬁndings
indicate that the GH genotypes of the animals could be associated with difference in the GH response in Japanese black cattle at 10
months of age. We also observed a relationship between genotype and animal performances, but other studies on more animals in
different conditions must be realized to make a deﬁnite conclusion.

1. Introduction
Bovine GH is a peptide hormone that consists of 191
amino acids [1]. The nucleotide sequence was originally
identiﬁed by Miller et al. [2]. Chikuni et al. [3,4] found
that cattle have single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNP)
at codon positions 127 and 172 on exon 5 of the bovine
GH gene. In homozygote, SNP at position 127 codes
for either leucine (A-allele; CTG) in the AA genotype

erozygote, which has both the A- and B-allele, is called
the AB genotype. Another SNP in the B genotype at the
position 172 is called the C genotype: threonine (Thr;
ACG) is replaced with methionine (Met; ATG). This
mutation was also reported in Japanese black [3] and
Angus [5] cattle.
Recently, evidence has been accumulating to suggest
that the mutation at position 127 affects the phenotype of
cattle. The distribution of the genotypes for the Leu/Val
polymorphism in the bovine GH gene is dependent on
breed, herd, province and country [3,6–8]. In Japanese
black cattle, it is thought that animals with the A-allele

have a greater body weight and rate of daily gain but

supported by the ﬁnding that plasma GH concentrations
are increased by GHRH administration (0.5 /H9262g/kg BW)
to a greater extent for the AA genotype than that for
BB genotype in Danish Jerseys at 6 months of age [7].
It seems reasonable to suggest that a greater sensitivity
to GHRH challenge would lead to a greater propensity
to catabolism, which may be advantageous for milk pro-
duction [9]. This assumption is supported by the ﬁndings
that the A-allele was most frequent in the Danish Hol-
stein breed (85%), relative to that in Danish Red and
Danish Jersey breeds [7], and that a signiﬁcant rela-
tionship was observed between its incidence and level
of milk production in Italian Simmental cattle [10].In
Japanese black cattle, the frequency of the A-allele was
44%, and lower than that of the B-allele (56%) [4], while
the frequency of the A-allele is much greater than that
of the B-allele in Holstein dairy cows. GH genotypes
may also affect plasma IGF-I levels because animals
with the AA genotype had the highest IGF-I concen-
trations in 11-month-old Polish Friesian cattle [11].In
order to determine whether the GH genotype is useful as
an indicator for animal’s performance, the relationship
between this polymorphism of the GH gene and animal
production needs to be further clariﬁed.
This is also the case for polymorphism of the GH
receptor gene because the mutations of the receptor are
demonstrated to cause growth retardation in humans due
to GH resistance or GH insensitivity [12]. Additionally,
we recently found Japanese black calves with Laron-
genotype growth retardation, caused by defects of their
GH receptors, which resulted in lower circulating IGF-I
levels despite higher GH levels than normal calves [13].
In the present study, we prepared 10-month-old
Japanese black cattle with three GH genotypes: AA, AB
and BB in order to determine the interaction between
body weight and plasma levels of basal and GHRH-
induced GH, basal insulin, IGF-I as well as other
metabolites. We used 10-month-old Japanese black cat-
tle because animals at this age are usually on the mar-
ket.

the “Guiding Principles for the Care and Use of Animals
in the Field of Physiological Sciences” (The Physiolog-
ical Society of Japan), and the present experiment was
approved by The Animal Care Committee of Tohoku
University.

2.1. Experimental design
More than 100 heads of Japanese black bull calves
were genotyped at the Hiroshima Prefectural Livestock
Technology Research Center for the Leu/Val polymor-
phism [14] before 10 months of age using white blood
cells. Thirty-one calves in total (10, 11 and 10 calves for
AA, AB and BB genotypes, respectively) were housed
in separate stalls and fed TMR ad libitum as shown
in Table 1. At 10 months of age, the calves were sub-
jected to blood sampling and a challenge with GHRH
after withdrawal of their feed for 24 h. They were ﬁtted
with a catheter (OD 1.20 mm) in the left jugular vein at
least 2 h before onset of blood sampling. For the experi-
ment to measure the basal GH, the blood samples (10 ml
each) were taken every 15 min over 6 h. For the exper-
iment to measure the GH increase induced by GHRH
challenge, blood samples were also taken at −30, −15,
0, 5, 10, 15, 30, 60, and 120 min following intravenous
injection of bovine GHRH (0.4 /H9262g/kg BW, Peptide Insti-
tute, Osaka, Japan) after blood sampling at time 0. Blood
samples were centrifuged at 8000 × g for 15 min at
4 ◦C, and blood plasma was divided into ﬁve portions
and stored at −30 ◦C until analyzed for hormones and
metabolites.

measured only for the samples at time 0. The leptin
concentration was measured by a RIA kit (for multi-
species, Linco, St. Louis, MO). The protein, glucose,

2.3. Statistics
The data are expressed as means ± S.E.M. For GH
analysis, the AUC (area under the curve) and IA (incre-
mental area over the basal release) were calculated and
expressed as ng min/ml.
The signiﬁcance of the difference among the treat-
ments was determined by Duncan’s test followed
by ANOVA. The linear regression analysis was also
employed for the interaction between IA and body
weight. When the probability of there being no differ-
ence between the means fell below 0.05, the difference
was considered to be signiﬁcant.
3. Results
Average body weights for the AA and AB genotypes
were signiﬁcantly greater than that for the BB genotype
(Table 2).
The plasma concentrations of insulin and IGF-I were
signiﬁcantly greater for the AA genotype than for the AB
genotype, or the AB and BB genotypes, respectively. On
the other hand, the plasma concentration of leptin was
signiﬁcantly greater for the BB genotype than for the AA
and AB genotypes.

the AB genotype. None of the other parameters mea-

4. Discussion
In the present study, we assessed the interaction
among GH gene polymorphism (AA, AB and BB geno-
types) and GH secretory function, and body weight in
Japanese black calves at 10 months of age. To our knowl-
edge, the present ﬁndings are the ﬁrst to clearly indicate
that the GH-IGF-I axis is different among these GH
genotypes in Japanese black calves. The body weight for
the BB genotype was signiﬁcantly lower than those for
the AA and AB genotypes. The area under the basal GH
concentration was signiﬁcantly greater for the AA geno-
type than for two other genotypes. In addition, the incre-
mental area over the basal GH concentrations in response
to intravenous GHRH injection (0.4 /H9262g/kg BW) was sig-
niﬁcantly smaller for the BB genotype than for two
other genotypes. It has also been shown that the plasma
concentration of GH in response to the GHRH admin-
istration (0.5 /H9262g/kg BW) for AA was greater than that
for the BB genotype in Danish Jerseys at 6 months of
age [7]. Plasma concentrations of IGF-I were greater for
the AA genotype than for the BB and AB genotypes
in the present study, which has also been reported in
Polish Friesian cattle [11].Geetal.[5] suggested that
serum IGF-I concentrations are associated with a single
nucleotide polymorphism in the promoter region of the
growth hormone receptor gene. The allele frequency of
the A and C genotypes consists of 80%, but that for the
B genotype does 20% of the total [3]. Taken together,
these ﬁndings suggest that GH secretion from the pitu-

However, the interaction was complex between
GHRH-induced GH secretion and body weight, because
there was a positive linear correlation (r = 0.6496,
P < 0.002) between these two parameters under an incre-
mental area of 600 ng min/ml, but a negative linear cor-
relation (r = 0.6473, P < 0.05) over 600 ng min/ml. These
ﬁndings suggest that the body weight of the animals
with the AB and BB genotypes, which showed smaller
response to GHRH stimulation than the AA genotype,
is increased by a mechanism being dependent on the
GHRH-induced GH response. However, the body weight
of the animals with AA genotype, which showed greater
response to GHRH stimulation than AB and BB geno-
types, is decreased by this GHRH-induced GH response.
In other words, over-secretion of GH, as was observed in
the animals with the AA genotype, causes a statistically
negative effect on the body weight, possibly due to the
catabolic actions of GH.
It is not clear at present whether or not an alteration in
the structure of GH due to the polymorphism can change
IGF-I production and release by the liver, or the mutant
is less capable of binding to the GH receptors of the
liver. An inability to bind to hepatic GH receptors may
cause growth retardation, as reported in humans, due to
GH resistance or GH insensitivity [12], and in Japanese
black calves with Laron-genotype growth retardation,
which have low circulating IGF-I levels despite high GH
levels [13].
The difference in GH structures due to SNP may affect
the sensitivity of the GH assay. This issue should be
clariﬁed in the near future with standard GH of the type
B. The plasma levels of IGF-I may suggest the difference
in validity of GH binding to its receptors on hepatocytes.
The present experiment has also demonstrated a neg-
ative relationship between plasma leptin concentrations
and body weight, as shown in Table 2. Despite some lim-
itations because of a low sensitivity of the antibody, the
usage of the multi-species leptin kit is as effective as an
ovine-speciﬁc RIA in determining relative leptin levels
in cattle [16]. Although it is not clear why there was a
negative relationship between these two parameters, a
higher level of circulating leptin level may reduce GH
release induced by GHRH stimulation as shown in ovine
somatotrophs [17], or it may be related with reduced food
intake in the BB genotype. In addition, it is not clear
at present whether or not marbling scores are related
with plasma leptin levels. However, our recent study sug-
gested that marbling scores for animals with the B- and
C-alleles are higher than those with the A-allele (our
unpublished data).
The average of plasma triglyceride concentration for
the BB genotype was greater than for the other geno-
types, while that of plasma cholesterol for the BB type
was less than for the other genotypes (Table 3). It is
unlikely that there is a correlation between these param-
eters and the GH genotype. Plasma insulin levels com-
monly change metabolic status in a reciprocal manner
against GH levels. In the present study, however, there
was not a clear relationship between plasma levels of
insulin and metabolites. This may be due to elevated
insulin and GH levels in the AA genotypes.
In summary, we have demonstrated that the GH geno-
types of the animals could be associated with difference
in the GH response. We also observed a relationship
between genotype and animal performances, but other
studies on more animals in different conditions must be
realized to make a deﬁnite conclusion.
