Tiller demography in tall fescue (Festuca arundinacea)
swards as inﬂuenced by nitrogen fertilization,
sowing method and grazing management

Instituto Nacional de Tecnologı´a Agropecuaria, Centro Regional Buenos Aires Norte, Estacio´ n Experimental
Agropecuaria Pergamino, Buenos Aires, Argentina
Abstract
Tall fescue is an important temperate grassland species
globally, but in many regions the effects of drought,
heat and poor grazing management, reduce its persis-
tence in summer. Furthermore, poor ground cover and
low tiller population density (TP) in winter result in low
radiation capture and thus limited potential to increase
the low growth rates at that time. Knowledge of the
effect of fertilization, defoliation managements and
establishment methods on demography of tall fescue
would help to improve sward management for better
persistence and winter growth. Accordingly, an exper-
iment was carried out in Pergamino, Argentina, to
study the effects of two N- fertilizer levels, two grazing
frequencies and two seeding row arrangements upon
tall fescue TP demography. Herbage mass, tiller appear-
ance (TA) and death rates were measured over 3 years.
Seasonal ﬂuctuations in TA and death rates determined
well-deﬁned periods of increase and decrease in TP
density. Maximum values were found at the end of
winter and minimum ones at the end of spring, after
the reproductive period. Nitrogen shortage and high
herbage mass limited TA. Tiller death rate followed TA
rate and was affected by nitrogen supply and defoliation
frequency in the same way that TA was. Sowing
arrangement affected tiller demography at the begin-
ning of the experiment.
Keywords: tall fescue, tiller demography, nitrogen,
sowing method, grazing management
Introduction
Tall fescue [Festuca arundinacea Schreb. also known as
Lolium arundinaceum Schreb. (Darbysh.) = Schedonorus
arundinaceus (Schreb.) Dumort] is an internationally
important temperate grass species. Its persistence is
impaired by biotic and abiotic stresses. Summer heat
and drought, along with poor grazing management, are
common causes of restriction of the lifespan of the
pasture (Milne, 2009).
In the humid Pampas of Argentina, tall fescue is
adapted to soils of low fertility and with risk of transient
waterlogging, which presents limitations for the
achievement of high crop yields. In this region, tall
fescue is present in 2Æ8 million ha of mixed swards
(INDEC, 2005) and also on an additional 3Æ0 million ha
of old pastures and roadsides. Most of the tall fescue in
the humid Pampas is from cultivars of north European
ecotypes, which stay green all year-round. Its peren-
nation strategy, through the maintenance of a high
tiller population (TP) density, permits tall fescue swards
to remain productive for more than 5 years, especially
in the southern part of the humid Pampas, where it
shows a better adaptation (Maddaloni and Ferrari,
2005). In contrast, in the northern areas of the Pampas,
which have warm summers and negative water bal-
ances from mid-December to mid-February, sward
persistence may be impaired if swards are not properly
managed during summer.
In winter, when tall fescue swards have low rates of
herbage growth, there are long periods during which
there is a high proportion of bare ground (Bertı´n, 2004)
and, as a consequence, there is low efﬁciency of light
capture and plant production. Winter herbage produc-
tion from grass swards is a key factor to the efﬁciency,
sustainability and stability of animal production sys-
tems. Farmers use oats or annual ryegrass crops to
overcome herbage shortage in winter, and on a dry-
matter basis, the cost is about 2Æ5 times more than that
of perennial pastures (Scheneiter, 2011). Winter crop

production for high levels of herbage production also
requires an early autumn sowing on good-quality soils,
conditions that are not always possible to provide.
Sward growth in winter is not increased signiﬁcantly
by the use of improved cultivars and ⁄ or fertilizater,
because the potential advantages of these alternatives
are limited by the inherent low temperatures and
radiation levels (Bertı´n, 2004). Another approach
would be to improve TP density, as this variable,
together with leaf number per tiller and leaf size,
determines the leaf area index (LAI) and hence sward
growth (Lemaire et al., 2009).
Tall fescue has a persistence strategy with a strong
dependence on reproductive development. It is charac-
terized by sward renewal from replacement tillers
originating at the base of harvested ﬂowering tillers
(Matthew et al., 1999) and by the seasonal ﬂuctuations
of solar radiation inputs (Gastal and Matthew, 2005).
Consistent with this, TP density of northern European
ecotypes has a peak in early spring in the humid
Pampas, just when shoot elongation begins, followed by
minimum values in early summer. During the following
autumn and winter, there is a period of slow recovery
of sward density (Bertı´n, 1988a).
Besides seasonal variations due to temperature and
light levels, TP density can be modiﬁed by defoliation
management, fertilization and sowing method. Canopy
leaf area, through its effect on the quality of light
(red ⁄ far red ratio) that reaches the base of the sward, is
a critical factor for the emergence of tillers (Casal et al.,
1985). Thus, changes in herbage mass, induced by
defoliation management, modify the population of
tillers in tall fescue, as they do with other species.
However, tall fescue shows less phenotypic plasticity
when compared, for example, with perennial ryegrass
(Lolium perenne L.). For this reason, tall fescue is
reported to have a greater critical sward height (sward
height that optimizes tillering) than perennial ryegrass
(Buttler and Hodgson, 1993; Tavakoli et al., 1993).
In the southern part of the humid Pampas, Mazzanti
et al. (1985) found that the positive response of tall
fescue tillering to nitrogen (N) fertilization relied upon
cultivar and defoliation severity. In other species, Cruz
and Boval (1999) showed that N fertilization increased
site ﬁlling, but that the N effect on tillering was because
of morphogenetic traits and LAI, which govern light
level and quality beneath the canopy (Simon and
Lemaire, 1987).
To overcome long periods when there is bare ground,
a close seeding arrangement might improve sward
density and, hence, light interception and sward pro-
duction. However, there is no local evidence to prove
this hypothesis (Scheneiter, 2007).
Throughout the year, changes in TP density are the
consequence of balancing tiller appearance (TA) and
tiller death (TD) (Matthew et al., 1999). The maximum
and minimum values of the two processes are decou-
pled for most of the year (Herna´ ndez Garay et al., 1997)
and result in the typical seasonal patterns of sward
density. In turn, TP shows a high variability between
years and defoliation systems (Bertı´n, 1988a). There-
fore, long-term studies of tiller survival within succes-
sive tiller cohorts conducted under grazing conditions
would achieve a better insight into tall fescue tiller
demography than the counting of tiller numbers at
regular intervals. From a practical point of view, these
studies permit the design of grazing-management
guidelines to improve the sustainability of pastoral
systems through sward persistence and productivity.
However, experiments with animals are expensive, are
time-consuming and require special infrastructure, and
therefore they are not commonly reported in the
literature (Matthew et al., 1999).
The objective of this experiment was to study, in tall
fescue swards, TP demography with different levels of N
supply, sowing methods and grazing managements. It is
expected that the information provided by this work
will contribute to the design of production and utiliza-
tion practices with a sound basis to improve tall fescue
winter production and sward persistence.
Materials and methods
Site description
The experiment was conducted in the northern part of
the humid Pampa at Pergamino Experimental Station
(33/C17652¢S, 60/C17633¢W; 68 m a.s.l.) on a Typic Argiudol soil,
Pergamino Series (INTA, 1972), with 4Æ15% organic
matter, 15 mg kg)1 phosphorus and pH 5Æ8 at the
beginning of the experiment.
Swards were sown on 27 April 2004 at a rate of 450
and 250 viable seeds m)2 of tall fescue and white clover
(Trifolium repens L.) respectively. The white clover
variety was a large-leaf type, cv. Lucero Plus INTA,
inoculated with Rhizobium leguminosarum bv trifolii
supplied by Rizobacter S.A (Pergamino, Bs As, Argen-
tina). The tall fescue was of north European ecotype, cv.
Palenque Plus INTA.
At seeding, 45 kg ha)1 of 18-46-0 (N-P-K) fertilizer
was applied in the seeding row. Weeds were prevented
by spraying with Flumetsulam (Dow AgroSciences,
Indianapolis, IN, USA) at 0Æ5Lha)1 at pre-emergence.
After sowing, swards were continuously grazed with
steers from early September to mid-October.
Long-term mean air temperature of the site is 10Æ4/C176C
in winter and 22Æ7/C176C in summer, and annual rainfall is
975 mm. Weather conditions during the experimental
period (December 2004 to January 2008) were charac-
terized by lower temperatures between May and

Treatments and experimental procedures
There were eight treatments resulting from the combi-
nation of two fertilizer N levels [0 and 200 kg N ha)1
year)1 (N) and N+)], two sowing methods [crossed (C)
and alternate (A) tall fescue-white clover rows] and two
grazing managements [frequent (F+) and infrequent
(F))].
Fertilization treatments began on 12 October when
108Æ3kgNha)1 were applied using UAN (urea and
ammonium nitrate) as the N source (30-0-0). From
then on, N was applied in N+ treatments at
200 kg N ha)1 twice a year (in March and August).
The two sowing methods were (i) drilling tall fescue
and white clover in alternate rows 16 cm apart (A) and
(ii) drilling tall fescue ﬁrst and then white clover at right
angles, in rows 16 cm apart (C). Sowing depth was
20 mm.
To generate contrasting amounts of herbage mass
throughout the experimental period, two contrasting
grazing management treatments were used: grazing
every 28 d (F+) or every 56 d (F)). Grazing was carried
out by crossbred steers independently in each paddock.
Each paddock was individually fenced and had an area
of 1400 m2. The total area of the experiment was
3Æ36 ha.
Grazing management treatments began in October
2004. The number of steers was adjusted in each
defoliation treatment to graze down to the targeted
residual sward height (70 mm) in 2–3 d. In 2007,
however, unfavourable weather conditions resulted in
low herbage mass and, for three of the grazing events,
steers were removed at a lower residual sward height
than the intended one. Grazing and pasture terminol-
ogy, where appropriate, follows Allen et al. (2011).
Treatments were arranged in a split–split plot design
with three replicates, which were seeding row arrange-
ments of whole plots, N level as a subplot and grazing
frequency a sub-subplot.
Measurements
Before grazing, herbage mass was assessed by harvest-
ing within ten rings each of 0Æ25 m2 per paddock.
Herbage was cut above 70 mm using hand-operated
sheep shears. Fresh herbage was weighed in the ﬁeld. A
representative sample was oven-dried in the laboratory
for 48 h at 60/C176C to determine dry-matter percentage
(Frame, 1993) and herbage mass calculated on a dry-
matter basis.
In each paddock, three ﬁxed frames of 0Æ048 m2
(15 cm · 32 cm) were positioned with the longest side
across either one (sowing treatment A) or two (treat-
ment C) of the tall fescue drill rows. To avoid bias, sites
at the paddock perimeter were excluded because these
were places where cattle rested and had access to water.
In December 2004, all the tillers inside the frames
were counted and tagged with coloured plastic rings.
From then on, every month until January 2008, tillers
that appeared during the previous month (age cohort)
were recorded and tagged with rings of different
colours. Dead tillers were also counted and their rings
removed (Korte et al., 1984; Jewiss, 1993). No distinc-
tion between daughter tillers, arising from either parent
tillers or rhizomes, was made. For each measurement
date, the tiller recording took 2–4 d.
Tiller demography
From January 2005 to January 2008, relative tiller
appearance rate (new tillers per 100 adult tillers per day,
TA) was calculated on a monthly basis, as the ratio
between new tillers and tillers present at the end of the
previous measurement period (Bahmani et al., 2003). As
an approximation, it was assumed that tillers appearing
during a given month did not produce daughter tillers
themselves within that month. For the same period,
relative death rate was calculated (dead tillers per 100
adult tillers per day, TD) as the ratio of dead tillers to the
tillers present at the beginning of the same measurement
period. For each age cohort, monthly tiller survival
probabilities (S) were calculated as the ratio of tillers at
the beginning of an observation period still present at
the end of the same period (Bahmani et al., 2003).
Tiller measurements were taken every month on the
same days for all grazing-management treatments. This
permitted the analysis of the effect of grazing manage-
ment on tiller dynamics at monthly intervals. As tiller
measurements were taken every 30 d, and grazing-
management treatments were set at 28- and 56-d
intervals, it follows that treatments and response
variables were out of phase. Theoretically, this could
have had implications on the results in the 56-d grazing
interval because two tiller measurements were taken
during that interval: one in the middle and another one
near the end. Two measurements, instead of one at the
end of the 56-d interval, would have little impact in
months of low rates of tiller turnover and sward
growth. However, in spring, with high rates of sward

Tiller population density was the addition of tiller
number of all cohorts that were present at a given time.
However, it should be noted that differences in forage
mass created by the grazing management might have
affected differentially the light levels beneath the canopy
when manipulating tillers for counting and tagging.
Hence, TP values might have been overestimated.
No attempt was made to measure directly the total
tiller density in separate areas because of the large
number of samples required each month to avoid the
spatial variability caused by grazing action.

data, no transformation was needed. All variables were

Results
Herbage mass
As herbage mass was estimated at different times for
each grazing management, no monthly comparisons
were made between F+ and F) treatments; instead,
results are presented separately for each grazing
management as an average of defoliation events
through the year. Winter herbage mass accumulation
with both grazing managements was also analysed.
To do this, herbage mass accumulations from June
to September were added (two and four values with
F) and F+ respectively) and then statistically
compared.
Under the F+ grazing-management treatment, herb-
age mass ranged from 360 (September 2007) to 2760 kg
DM ha)1 (June 2005). Nitrogen fertilization increased
the herbage mass by 55% (649 vs. 1013 kg DM ha)1,
for N) and N+; P <0Æ001). C treatments occasionally
showed greater herbage mass with N+ treatments than
A treatments (the interaction fertilization · sowing
method was signiﬁcant). On average throughout the
experimental period, herbage mass was 15% greater for
sowing treatment C than treatment A.
Under the F) grazing-management treatment, herb-
age mass ranged from 580 (August 2006) to
3800 kg DM ha)1 (May 2005). Nitrogen fertilization
increased herbage mass by 75% (1178 vs. 2064 kg
DM ha)1, for N) and N+; P <0Æ001). Sowing method
had only a small effect on herbage mass. In April–May
2007, herbage mass was greater with the C and N+
combination than with other treatments (interaction N
fertilization · sowing arrangement; P <0Æ05).
In the winter of 2005, herbage mass accumulation
was greater with F+ (P <0Æ01) and with N+ (P <0Æ001),
compared with the F) and N) treatments (3514 vs.
3078 and 4209 vs. 2383 kg DM ha)1).
In 2006, an interaction of grazing management · N
fertilization was detected (P <0Æ004) when the N
fertilization effect was higher at the frequent grazing
(1274, 3193, 730 and 1817 kg DM ha)1 for F+N),
F+N+, F)N) and F)N) respectively). There was also
greater accumulation of herbage mass with sowing
treatment C than with A (1903 vs. 1605 kg DM ha)1,
P <0Æ03).
In 2007, winter rainfall was very low and therefore
low rates of herbage growth were obtained. Experi-
mental errors were high and no differences between
treatments were detected.
Tiller population density
Tiller population density had peaks from August to
September and minimum values from January to
March. Sowing method and N fertilization were the
main factors that affected TP. Interactions of N fertil-
ization with sowing method and grazing management
were infrequent. Interactions of sowing method with
grazing management and triple interactions were not
detected.

Relative tiller appearance rate
Relative TA rate was low in summer (from November to
February). From then on, TA increased steadily to reach
peaks in winter (July and August). This general pattern
did not occur in 2006 because of a drought between
May and September.
Nitrogen fertilization was the main factor affecting TA
(Table 2). Signiﬁcant N fertilization · grazing manage-
ment interactions frequently occurred during the
experiment. In all cases, the F+ N+ combination had a
higher TA than the N) treatments and, in some
months, than the other treatments. During most of
the experiment, N) F+ had the lowest TA values.
Signiﬁcant sowing method x N fertilization interac-
tions were detected several times throughout the
experiment (Table 2). In 2006, treatment N+ with C
had the highest TA values, whereas in 2007, the
treatment N) with A had the lowest TA values.

Discussion
Herbage mass and tiller demography may be different
depending on whether grazing is set at ﬁxed intervals or
by state variables of the sward (pre-grazing height, LAI,
or herbage mass) for which soil and climatic environ-
ment determines the frequency of grazing. Therefore, at
times of the year with low growth rates of the sward,
grazing frequency based on ﬁxed intervals would be
greater than with the pre-grazing target criterion.
Lower rates of TA and ⁄ or higher TD would be expected
if, for example, assimilates are in short supply with the
ﬁxed-interval criterion. In contrast, during times of the
year with high growth rates and reproductive develop-
ment, the grazing frequency with the ﬁxed-interval

Herbage mass
For both 28- and 56-d (F) and F+) defoliation
frequencies, herbage mass values were increased two-
fold where N was applied. In the same way, F) herbage
mass was twofold greater than F+, regardless of the N
fertilization level. Consequently, herbage mass on offer,
as averaged over the whole experimental period, was
similar under both defoliation managements because,
through the year, the number of grazing events with F+
was double that of F). For the whole experimental
period, herbage mass for the F+ ⁄ F) or for the N) ⁄ N+
ratios, had values of 0Æ5. On an annual basis, for
approximately similar dates of defoliation (May 2005 to
March 2006, April 2006 to February 2007 and March to
December 2007), the F+ ⁄ F) ratios were 0Æ74 ± 0Æ001
and 0Æ65 ± 0Æ04 for N) and N+ respectively (averaging
2005 ⁄ 2006 and 2006 ⁄ 2007). However, in 2007, F+ ⁄ F)
ratios were 0Æ19 ± 0Æ01 and 0Æ23 ± 0Æ06 for N) and N+
respectively, showing a better performance of frequent
defoliation in years with more favourable rainfall
(2005 ⁄ 2006 and 2006 ⁄ 2007).
In the winter period, the previous relationship
between grazing management and herbage mass was
not found. Increased herbage mass, either by grazing
management or by N fertilization, was related to higher
TPs. According to the size ⁄ density compensation the-
ory, this is possible when herbage mass is low (Matthew
et al., 1995) as it is the case in winter months. In spring
and autumn, when high herbage mass usually occurs,
low density of large tillers, or vice versa, could be
expected and as a consequence no changes in herbage
mass because of tiller density may be possible. Inspec-
tion of results presented by Xia et al. (1990) working
with perennial ryegrass shows the same relationship. In
their study, autumn–winter pasture net production was
related to TP density (P <0Æ04, r2 =0Æ90), whereas in
spring it was not (P <0Æ50).
Sowing method had quantitatively small, but strate-
gically important effects on herbage mass. The cross-
drilled rows showed small advantages in terms of
herbage mass compared with alternately sown rows,
but only with N fertilization and frequent grazing.
Therefore, in months of low herbage growth rates,
swards grazed frequently and adequately supplied with
N can capture more light early, if the tall fescue drill
rows are 16 cm instead of 32 cm apart. In contrast, in
months when herbage growth rates are high and the
sward has longer rest periods between grazing events
(the F) treatments), this process does not happen. In
the above situation, senescence might neutralize the
earlier advantages in herbage growth of swards
obtained with the close-spaced seed drilling arrange-
ment. Although seed drilling arrangement has been a
matter of much research in Argentina (Scheneiter,

Tiller population density
Tiller population densities throughout this experiment,
as measured inside ﬁxed frames, were higher than the
values previously reported, where all tillers were
recorded at random sites over time (Bertı´n, 1988a).
Discrepancies between methodologies have been attrib-
uted to bias in site selection when frames were placed
and disturbances occurred during tiller counting and
tagging (Herna´ ndez Garay et al., 1997; Bahmani et al.,
2003).
Tiller population densities and related variables refer
to tillers originated from both the axillary buds of
parent tillers and rhizomes. Tillers arising from rhi-
zomes were found in August and September, whereas
tillers arising from ‘stolon-like’ structures (Brock et al.,
1997) were seldom seen.
Essentially, patterns of variation in TP throughout the
year and its associated processes were the same for all
treatments, but their magnitudes were different. The
peak of TP density in spring (August and September)
was consistent with previous TA rates of 0Æ53 ± 0Æ26 for
N) and of 0Æ90 ± 0Æ36 for the N+ treatments. In
contrast, TD rates were low and dominated by N
level · grazing management and N level · sowing
method interactions. Interestingly, frequent defoliation
had both higher and smaller TP densities, depending on
N fertilization. When N supply does not limit sward
growth, frequent defoliation may prevent excessive
shading at the base of the canopy and thereby encour-
age tillering. When the N supply is limited, leaf
expansion and thus the available assimilate are also
restricted. In this situation, if defoliation is frequent,
assimilates would be preferentially directed to expand
new leaf tissue rather than initiating new tillers. In
addition to low assimilate partitioning to TA owing to
source limitation, the lack of N stimulus would be the
cause of the decrease in TP with F+ N) throughout the
experiment (Matthew et al., 1999). In this regard, tall
fescue has been shown to be a particularly sensitive
species to allocate assimilates to tillering when resource
availability is restricted (Belesky et al., 2011). There is
also evidence that lateral expansion of the tussock by
tillers arising from rhizomes might be restricted under
such conditions (Brock et al., 1997).
The lowest TP densities in early summer were
coincident with intermediate values of TD rates in the
previous months but principally with very low rates of
TA. Yeh et al. (1976) found that in tall fescue, high
temperatures in summer were associated with low
tillering and high auxin concentrations at tiller bases,
whereas at low temperatures, the reverse was true.
They suggested that critical levels of stored assimilates
and concentrations of endogenous growth regulators in
stem bases could be involved in low TA in summer.
Rainfall also affected TP in early summer; for example,
the lowest TP values in N+F+ were recorded in years
with below-average rainfall.
The changes in TP through the experiment provide
evidence for the nutritional limitations of tall fescue
swards when they are not supplied with adequate N
levels. It is known that improvement of N captured by
the sward induces a higher ‘site ﬁlling’ and higher
tillering rates (Cruz and Boval, 1999). In this way, the
TP pattern in the N) treatments was nearly constant
across the years, whereas with N+, the TP increased
from 2005 to 2007. The N effect was more evident with
frequent grazing than infrequent grazing, because high
herbage mass with the F) N+ treatments prevented
tillering. In contrast, with F+ N+ treatment, more light
reached the canopy base and tillering was encouraged
(Simon and Lemaire, 1987).
In relation to drought stress, Milne (2009) pointed
out that grazing during low rain periods is the most
critical factor that may decrease tall fescue persistence.
Better performance with F) treatments at the end of
the experiment could be associated with low rainfall
and temperatures during 2007.
Unlike perennial ryegrass, tillering in tall fescue is
encouraged by grazing at a sward height of no lower
than 5 cm because of its inherent lower tiller density,
higher tiller size and lower leaves, and TA rates (Buttler
and Hodgson, 1993). In the same way, Asay et al.
(1977) found that in tall fescue, both tillering and
stored assimilates were raised when defoliation height
increased from 4 to 12 cm, and Belesky et al. (2011)
also reported similar ﬁndings when defoliation height
in tall fescue was increased from 5 to 10 cm. That
critical grazing height was not always avoided in 2007
owing to the difﬁculties in controlling the targeted post-
grazing height with such low herbage mass.
Relative tiller appearance and death rates
Tiller appearance and TD values were lower than the
ones reported by Herna´ ndez Garay et al. (1997) in
perennial ryegrass, conﬁrming the inherent lower
tillering turnover in tall fescue when compared with
that species.
Seasonal decline in TA rates began with the onset of
the reproductive stage, when the growth and develop-
ment of ﬂowering shoots were the main fate of
assimilates. In addition, environmental incidence in
TA was evident over the whole experiment and, for
example, low rainfall during October 2005 and August–

(0Æ21 ± 0Æ04 and 0Æ37 ± 0Æ14 respectively). Bahmani
et al. (2003) reported signiﬁcant but occasional irriga-
tion effects on TA in perennial ryegrass. Peaks of TA
were consistent with low herbage mass, in agreement
with the negative relationship between LAI and TA
found in tall fescue by Gastal and Matthew (2005).
At the beginning of the experiment, the alternate
sowing arrangement had higher light capture and
lateral space on tiller bases when compared with
crossed arrangement. This fact probably determined
an advantage in TA for the alternate sowing method.
The tall fescue rows enlarged with the passage of time
and that advantage therefore disappeared.
The pattern of TD showed a lag with respect to TA
pattern although treatments effects were quite similar
in both variables. This result was in agreement with
that reported by Herna´ ndez Garay et al. (1997). In this
way, from October to March, TD exceeded TA and the
opposite result occurred from April to September. These
ﬂuctuations determined well-deﬁned periods of in-
crease and decrease in TP. Gastal and Matthew (2005)
found that high values of TD were partly related to
transition from the vegetative to the reproductive stage
in both Italian ryegrass and tall fescue. Also, they stated
that water stress in summer kills daughter tillers if they
do not develop enough roots, and hence high TD values
might be expected in that season. In this experiment,
although relatively high TD values were recorded in
summer, after the reproductive period, the highest TD
values might have been caused by the death of
reproductive tillers from October to December. In
agreement with that, Bertı´n (1988b) found that most
tall fescue TA from March to June was reproductive and
those cohorts had also lower survival probabilities in
November, a month with the highest TD.

In general, the range of S values, from 0Æ57 to 0Æ95,
coincides with that of 0Æ58–0Æ98 reported by Bahmani

in autumn months, had lower S values in November,

Conclusions
Sowing method and N fertilization · grazing manage-
ment interaction have important implications on TP
density of tall fescue swards during most of the year.
Sowing method, by means of its effect on TA rate, has a
signiﬁcant effect on tiller density, but not on herbage
mass. Nitrogen status of tall fescue swards determines
the grazing management effects on TP density: frequent
grazing promotes tillering under conditions with ade-
quate N supply, whereas it was impaired under condi-
tions of N deﬁciency. High TP density in winter is a key
requirement to obtain more herbage mass during this
season. However, in the north of Buenos Aires Prov-
ince, frequent grazing with low herbage mass in
summer implies damage to tall fescue sward structure
and in sward persistence.
